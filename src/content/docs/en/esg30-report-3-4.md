## Executive Summary

Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) is an indispensable technological anchor for deep industrial decarbonization. However, the global CCUS ecosystem currently confronts a substantial "delivery gap" — grandiose long-term projections contrast sharply with sluggish final investment decision (FID) conversion rates. The central barrier has fundamentally shifted from single-point engineering feasibility to **multi-stakeholder industrial coordination obstacles and missing governance infrastructures**.

This report introduces a comprehensive governance framework bridging physical engineering facts, policy instruments, and institutional applications:

1. **Systemic Bottlenecks & Governance Shift**: Analysis of capture-stage energy penalty, source-network-sink coordination dilemmas, and long-term geological stewardship liabilities.
2. **7-Jurisdiction Benchmark Matrix**: Comparative evaluation across the US, EU, UK, Canada (Alberta), Norway, and China, highlighting differentiated market and regulatory trajectories.
3. **Continuous dMRV Evidence Architecture**: A 4-tier network translating raw IoT sensor telemetry into verifiable, tamper-proof carbon accounting assets for carbon credit issuance, transition finance, and regulatory compliance.
4. **Empirical Case Analyses**: Quest (Canada), Northern Lights (Europe), Dayawan (China), and Daqing EOR field implementations.
5. **Actionable Policy Recommendations**: Upstream evidence requirements in cluster selection, standardized data interfaces, and empirical shadow assessments for post-closure liability succession.
